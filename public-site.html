Section 3 · Technical Note

How the Bureau scores a hamburger

The Official Rankings are not a matter of opinion. They are several matters of opinion, entered to one decimal place and combined by a fixed procedure, applied identically to every establishment.

  1. 1

    Independent examination

    Each audit is one examiner, one visit, one burger, at one location. Examiners file independently. They need not visit the same branch, order the same burger or attend on the same day, and no examiner may alter another’s scores.

  2. 2

    Scoring

    Seven categories are scored from 0.0 to 10.0, in tenths. Six are entered directly. Service is never entered: it is derived as the mean of two sub-scores, Speed and Friendliness. An audit’s weighted score is the weighted mean of all seven categories, at the official weights in Table 4.

  3. 3

    One examiner, one vote

    Where an examiner has audited an establishment more than once, their audits there are averaged first. Each examiner then carries exactly one vote per establishment, however many times they returned. Repeat visits refine an examiner’s finding; they do not multiply it (Illustration 1).

  4. 4

    Certification

    An establishment enters the Official Rankings once two distinct active examiners hold a current-schema audit for it. Every further examiner who files joins the aggregate on equal terms. A current-schema audit records every category score, both Service sub-scores, a location and the burger examined. Filings made under an earlier schema are preserved exactly as filed but no longer count, and are never assumed to be zero.

  5. 5

    The index

    The Composite Patty Index (CPI) is the mean of the examiners’ weighted scores, multiplied by ten: a figure from 0 to 100. All arithmetic is carried at full precision. Figures are rounded to one decimal place for display only, and a rounded figure never decides an order.

  6. 6

    Alternate ranking bases

    Burger Quality Index (BQI) asks which establishment serves the best hamburger if the fries, the value and the service are disregarded. It uses the burger categories at their official relative weights, renormalized to 100%, with the same examiner balancing and the same 0–100 scale.

    Fries ranks certified establishments on the combined examiner mean for the Fries category alone, on the ten-point scale. It is a category score, not an index, and the Bureau does not call it CPI.

    Neither is a separate certification. An establishment is ranked on every basis or on none.

      Section 5 · Institutional History

      About the Bureau

      The Bureau of Patty Statistics began as two people who had eaten a great many hamburgers and could no longer tolerate the absence of rigorous measurement. Its earliest proceedings consisted of a scoring sheet, two examiners and a disagreement about bun structure that remains, in several respects, unresolved.

      Over the objections of no one, the exchange of opinions was formalized: a weighted scoring schema, a certification requirement and a permanent register, followed in due course by a controlled taxonomy of establishment classes and a shared registry of locations. The Bureau has since grown into a multi-examiner institution with an Office of Auditor Accountability and a Records Office whose contents it declines to discuss.

      The Bureau receives no public funding, holds no regulatory authority and has never been asked for its opinion. It publishes anyway.

      Charter Articles of Practice

      1. I

        Every hamburger receives due process.

        No finding is official until a second examiner has filed.

      2. II

        No examiner outvotes the Bureau by eating more.

        Repeat visits are averaged before examiners are.

      3. III

        Precision is not optional.

        Scores are entered to the tenth, computed in full and disputed to the hundredth.

      4. IV

        History is not rewritten.

        Earlier filings are preserved as filed and never quietly zero-filled.

      5. V

        Fries are measured, not assumed.

        They are also not burgers.
